# Rewrite rulebook

Each rule names its source and whether it is expert advice (E) or measured outcome data (M). Exemplar resumes the skill scans are "expert-endorsed", never "outcome-proven": no public dataset links resume text to hiring outcomes.

## Rejection triggers (fix before anything else) — Bock, E
1. Typos and grammar errors.
2. Length: one page per 10 years of experience, two pages maximum.
3. Formatting that breaks parsing or reading: columns, tables for layout, text boxes, graphics, tiny fonts. Use one column.
4. Confidential information (current employer figures that are not public, client names without public reference).
5. Anything untrue or untraceable. Here: every line resolves to a ledger entry.

## Bullet construction — Bock, E
6. "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]": start with a verb, measure the result, give a baseline, say how.
7. Two to five bullets per role for the last 10 years; older roles may compress to one line.
8. Every role carries a one-line company context and a scope line (people, budget, revenue).

## The six-second zone — E
9. The top third (name, headline, profile, first role) must name the target role, the segment and one proof number. The advocate test (rubric 19) checks it.

## ATS — E
10. Plain section headers (Experience, Education, Skills). Dates as "Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY". Standard fonts. The rendered PDF must extract in reading order (gate `ats_parse`).
11. Use the niche's exact title words and top required terms, in context — never a keyword block.

## 2026 conditions — M unless marked
12. AI sameness: recruiters report AI-written resumes look nearly identical; 80% of surveyed hiring managers say they can spot one (E, survey). → rubric 18.
13. Tailor per niche, not per posting: one master ledger, one version per niche, headline/profile/first bullets swapped (E).
14. Proof beats claims at senior level: competence is assumed; the profile must be easy to advocate for (E, IMD).
