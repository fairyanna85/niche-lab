# Design rubric for the independent review

The reviewer receives screenshots only (one per act, 390 and 1440, dark and light), this rubric, and one paragraph on who reads the report: a job seeker, on a phone as often as a laptop, reading about themselves. The reviewer has not seen the code or the plan's reasoning.

Score each act 1–5 on each criterion. Any score under 4 must come with a fix written as an instruction ("move X", "cut Y", "make Z the largest element"), not as a feeling.

| Criterion | 5 looks like | 2 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchy | One thing is obviously the point of the screen; the eye lands there first | Several elements compete; the headline is not the largest thing |
| Rhythm | Spacing, alignment and widths repeat; edges line up across panels and buttons | Ragged edges, uneven gaps, buttons of different sizes side by side |
| Legibility | Every label readable without zooming; contrast comfortable in both themes | Small grey text on dark, labels crowded or cut |
| Signature visual | The act's one memorable visual is given room and reads at a glance | The signature visual is small, cluttered, or missing |
| Continuity | The act clearly follows from the one before and hands over to the next | The act could be in any order; no sense of a journey |
| Pull | "I would keep reading": the act makes the reader want the next one | Feels like a dashboard dump or a template |

Also report, separately: anything that looks broken (overlap, cut text, empty panels), and the single change that would most improve the whole report.
